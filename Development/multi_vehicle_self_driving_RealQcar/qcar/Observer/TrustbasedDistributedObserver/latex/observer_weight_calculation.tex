\section{Purpose}

This note summarizes the weighting rule used by the trust-aware distributed
observer. The presentation is intentionally simpler than the software
implementation: it keeps the main mathematical structure and collects secondary
implementation safeguards into compact terms. The objective is to obtain a
row-stochastic set of weights for each host vehicle \(i\) and target vehicle
\(j\), so that reliable direct measurements and trusted neighbor estimates
receive higher influence while unreliable information is automatically
attenuated.

\section{Notation}

Let \(i\) denote the host vehicle that runs the observer and let \(j\) denote
the target vehicle being estimated. At time \(k\),
\(\hat{\bm{x}}_{i}^{j}(k)\in\R^{n}\) is the current estimate of target \(j\)
stored by host \(i\). The target's direct V2V measurement is denoted by
\(\bm{z}_{j}(k)\), and \(\hat{\bm{x}}_{\ell}^{j}(k)\) denotes neighbor
\(\ell\)'s estimate of target \(j\).

The trust model provides two types of scores:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \(T_{i,\ell}(k)\in[0,1]\): host \(i\)'s trust in neighbor
    \(\ell\) as an information source.
    \item \(L_{i,j}(k)\in[0,1]\): local/direct trust in target \(j\)'s
    broadcast measurement.
\end{itemize}
The design parameters are the trust threshold \(\theta_T\), the maximum number
of neighbors \(\kappa\), the nominal direct-measurement gain \(a_0\), the
nominal self/prediction gain \(a_s\), the nominal neighbor budget
\(a_N=1-a_0-a_s\), and the maximum per-neighbor weight \(w_{\max}\). In the
current configuration, typical values are \(a_0=0.4\), \(a_s=0.2\),
\(a_N=0.4\), \(\theta_T=0.5\), and \(w_{\max}=0.4\).

\section{Trusted Information Set}

For each target \(j\), host \(i\) first selects the neighbors that both provide
an estimate of \(j\) and pass the trust threshold:
\begin{equation}
    \Lset_i^j(k)
    =
    \TopK
    \left\{
        \ell \in \N_i \setminus \{j\}
        \; \middle| \;
        \hat{\bm{x}}_{\ell}^{j}(k) \ \text{is available},
        \ T_{i,\ell}(k) \geq \theta_T
    \right\},
    \label{eq:trusted_set}
\end{equation}
where \(\TopK\{\cdot\}\) keeps at most \(\kappa\) neighbors, sorted by
descending trust. The target \(j\) itself is not used as a neighbor source for
estimating \(j\); its information enters only through the direct measurement
channel.

The selected neighbor trust scores are normalized as
\begin{equation}
    b_{i,\ell}^{j}(k)
    =
    \frac{T_{i,\ell}(k)}
    {\sum_{r\in\Lset_i^j(k)} T_{i,r}(k)},
    \qquad
    \ell\in\Lset_i^j(k),
    \label{eq:neighbor_basis}
\end{equation}
when the denominator is nonzero. These normalized values determine how the
neighbor budget is distributed.

\section{Raw Channel Scores}

The observer uses three information channels: direct measurement, self
prediction, and neighbor consensus. Define
\[
    \chi_{i,j}(k)=
    \begin{cases}
        1, & \text{if the direct measurement } \bm{z}_j(k) \text{ is available and accepted},\\
        0, & \text{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
\]
The raw channel scores are
\begin{align}
    \bar{w}_{0,i}^{j}(k)
    &=
    a_0 \, \chi_{i,j}(k) \, L_{i,j}(k) \, \phi_0^j(k),
    \label{eq:raw_direct}
    \\
    \bar{w}_{s,i}^{j}(k)
    &=
    a_s \, \rho_{s,i}^{j}(k) \, \phi_s^j(k),
    \label{eq:raw_self}
    \\
    \bar{w}_{i,\ell}^{j}(k)
    &=
    a_N \, b_{i,\ell}^{j}(k) \, \phi_N^j(k),
    \qquad \ell\in\Lset_i^j(k).
    \label{eq:raw_neighbor}
\end{align}
Here \(w_0\) weights the direct measurement, \(w_s\) is the residual
self/prediction weight, and \(w_{i,\ell}^{j}\) weights neighbor \(\ell\)'s
estimate of target \(j\). The factors \(\phi_0^j,\phi_s^j,\phi_N^j\) are
compact confidence multipliers derived from the attack flags; they are equal to
one during normal operation. The self confidence \(\rho_{s,i}^{j}\in[0,1]\)
can be chosen from the self-consistency score of the trust model. A practical
choice is
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{s,i}^{j}(k)
    =
    \gamma_{\min} + (1-\gamma_{\min})\gamma_{s,i}^{j}(k),
    \label{eq:self_confidence}
\end{equation}
where \(\gamma_{s,i}^{j}\in[0,1]\) is the self-consistency score and
\(\gamma_{\min}\) prevents the self channel from vanishing.

\section{Normalization and Influence Cap}

The raw scores are normalized jointly:
\begin{equation}
    S_i^j(k)
    =
    \bar{w}_{0,i}^{j}(k)
    +
    \bar{w}_{s,i}^{j}(k)
    +
    \sum_{\ell\in\Lset_i^j(k)} \bar{w}_{i,\ell}^{j}(k).
\end{equation}
If \(S_i^j(k)>0\), the preliminary weights are
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{w}_{q,i}^{j}(k)
    =
    \frac{\bar{w}_{q,i}^{j}(k)}{S_i^j(k)},
    \qquad
    q\in\{0,s\}\cup\Lset_i^j(k).
    \label{eq:prelim_weights}
\end{equation}
If \(S_i^j(k)=0\), the observer falls back to the self channel:
\(w_{s,i}^{j}(k)=1\).

To prevent one neighbor from dominating the update, each neighbor weight is
capped:
\begin{equation}
    w_{i,\ell}^{j}(k) \leq w_{\max},
    \qquad \ell\in\Lset_i^j(k).
    \label{eq:cap_constraint}
\end{equation}
Any excess weight is redistributed among the remaining uncapped neighbors in
proportion to \(b_{i,\ell}^{j}\). If no uncapped neighbor remains, the excess is
assigned to the self channel. Finally,
\begin{equation}
    w_{s,i}^{j}(k)
    =
    1
    -
    w_{0,i}^{j}(k)
    -
    \sum_{\ell\in\Lset_i^j(k)} w_{i,\ell}^{j}(k),
    \label{eq:self_residual}
\end{equation}
which gives the row-stochastic property
\begin{equation}
    w_{0,i}^{j}(k)
    +
    w_{s,i}^{j}(k)
    +
    \sum_{\ell\in\Lset_i^j(k)} w_{i,\ell}^{j}(k)
    =
    1.
    \label{eq:row_stochastic}
\end{equation}

\section{Observer Update}

Using the weights above, the consensus-corrected estimate is written as
\begin{equation}
    \bm{x}_{c,i}^{j}(k)
    =
    w_{s,i}^{j}(k)\hat{\bm{x}}_{i}^{j}(k)
    +
    w_{0,i}^{j}(k)\bm{z}_{j}(k)
    +
    \sum_{\ell\in\Lset_i^j(k)}
    w_{i,\ell}^{j}(k)\hat{\bm{x}}_{\ell}^{j}(k).
    \label{eq:convex_update}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, this can be written in correction form as
\begin{equation}
    \bm{x}_{c,i}^{j}(k)
    =
    \hat{\bm{x}}_{i}^{j}(k)
    +
    w_{0,i}^{j}(k)
    \left(\bm{z}_{j}(k)-\hat{\bm{x}}_{i}^{j}(k)\right)
    +
    \sum_{\ell\in\Lset_i^j(k)}
    w_{i,\ell}^{j}(k)
    \left(\hat{\bm{x}}_{\ell}^{j}(k)-\hat{\bm{x}}_{i}^{j}(k)\right).
    \label{eq:correction_update}
\end{equation}
The corrected state is then propagated through the target dynamics:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\bm{x}}_{i}^{j}(k+1)
    =
    f_j\!\left(
        \bm{x}_{c,i}^{j}(k),
        \bm{u}_{j}(k),
        T_s
    \right),
    \label{eq:prediction}
\end{equation}
where \(f_j(\cdot)\) is the discrete-time vehicle model and \(T_s\) is the
sampling period.

\section{Paper-Style Simplified Variant}

For a more compact theoretical presentation, the same idea can be expressed as
an equal-weight rule over a legitimate information set. Define
\begin{equation}
    \T_i^j(k)
    =
    \Lset_i^j(k)
    \cup
    \left\{0 \; \middle| \; \chi_{i,j}(k)=1,\ L_{i,j}(k)\geq\theta_T\right\},
\end{equation}
where the index \(0\) denotes the direct measurement channel. Let
\begin{equation}
    n_i^j(k)=\max\left\{\kappa,\ |\T_i^j(k)|+1\right\}.
\end{equation}
Then every legitimate external channel receives
\begin{equation}
    w_{q,i}^{j}(k)
    =
    \frac{1}{n_i^j(k)},
    \qquad q\in\T_i^j(k),
\end{equation}
and the remaining mass is assigned to the self channel:
\begin{equation}
    w_{s,i}^{j}(k)
    =
    1
    -
    \sum_{q\in\T_i^j(k)} w_{q,i}^{j}(k).
\end{equation}
This form is less adaptive than the trust-proportional rule, but it is useful
for stability analysis because all accepted external sources have bounded and
uniform influence.

\section{Summary}

The implemented observer can therefore be interpreted as a trust-weighted
convex fusion rule. Direct measurements are weighted by their local trust,
neighbor estimates are weighted by source trust, every neighbor is capped to
avoid dominance, and the remaining mass stays with the host's own predicted
state. This gives a simple, row-stochastic observer update that is suitable for
both implementation and academic presentation.
